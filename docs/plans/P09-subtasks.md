# P09 — Hardening, NDPA & launch: tasks and defaults

The tasks of [IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md § 3](../../IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md) P09, and the defaults each task agent took where the docs left a choice open, for the product owner to confirm. None of them changes a state machine (AGENT.md § 8): where an erasure meets a record with a status (a lease, a bill, a payment, a subscription), the status is read, never changed.

Rules: AGENT.md § 3 (invariants), § 7 (prohibitions); [INSTALLER.md](../../INSTALLER.md); [PLATFORM_CONSOLE.md](../../PLATFORM_CONSOLE.md); the runbooks in [docs/runbooks/](../runbooks/).

## P09-02 — NDPA 2023 + GAID 2025

Built 2026-11-04: consent records, the privacy policy and terms pages (versioned, with the data protection officer and what a landlord sees), data exports of a tenant or an organization, erasure of a tenant and purge of a cancelled organization after the 90-day retention window, a breach register with the 72-hour deadline, and [docs/runbooks/breach-response.md](../runbooks/breach-response.md). How it is built: [ARCHITECTURE.md § 10 "How the NDPA tooling is built"](../../ARCHITECTURE.md), [PLATFORM_CONSOLE.md § 11](../../PLATFORM_CONSOLE.md), [DATABASE_SCHEMA.md](../../DATABASE_SCHEMA.md) `consent_records`, `data_requests`, `data_breaches`.

### Defaults taken in P09-02 (agent, 2026-11-04; for the product owner to confirm)

**Consent and the legal pages**

1. **Consent at signup is the existing notice, recorded.** The account form already says "By creating an account you agree to the Terms and Privacy policy"; submitting it now writes two consent records (terms, privacy) with the versions, IP address and browser. No required tick box was added, so signup works as before. Accepting a team, tenant or owner invitation records the same, with the organization; the "join" buttons for people who already have an account now carry the same notice.
2. **The default privacy policy and terms are a starting draft**, written for NDPA 2023 and GAID 2025 (`resources/legal/*.html`), editable in Console → Settings → Public website as before. The terms name the customer (the agency or landlord) as the reporting entity under the Money Laundering (Prevention and Prohibition) Act 2022 and state that GoProperty support may view accounts (PLATFORM_CONSOLE.md § 6). **A lawyer must review both before launch** (launch checklist, IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md § 4).
3. **A version is a fingerprint of the published text**: `v-` and the first 12 characters of its SHA-256, shown at the foot of each page and kept on each consent record. Any change is a new version. Nobody is asked to agree again when the text changes (follow-up).
4. **Product email is opt-in only**: an unticked box at signup ("Email me product news and tips") and a switch in Settings → Notifications, each change a consent record; off until switched on. No product email is sent yet.
5. **What the landlord sees is fixed wording on the privacy page**, outside the editable text, so it cannot be dropped by mistake (the P03-16 decision, [owner-visibility-2026-10-29.md](owner-visibility-2026-10-29.md) item 6): name, phone, email, address, occupation, emergency contact, KYC status, ID type with the number's last four characters, leases, payments and receipts, and the deposit.
6. **The data protection officer** is four settings (`privacy.dpo_name`, `_email`, `_phone`, `_address`) in Console → Settings → Data protection (`privacy.settings`), shown on the privacy page; name and email both or neither. Unset, the page points at the support email.

**Exports**

7. **Who may export.** GoProperty staff with `privacy.requests` (compliance-officer, super-admin), for any organization or tenant, on a request whose identity they checked; and an organization's owners (`privacy.export`), for their own organization or one of its tenants (the organization is the controller answering its tenant). Not administrators or agents. Never during a support session; allowed while the organization is suspended or cancelled.
8. **A tenant's export is per organization.** A person renting from two agencies gets two separate exports; each holds only that agency's rows. Their sign-in account and consent records made outside that organization are not in it.
9. **What a tenant's export holds:** their rows in the 33 tables PersonalDataMap lists as tenants' (files included) (tenant record, leases, deposits, contracts, bills and items, payments and allocations, receipts, proofs, payment notices, Paystack transactions and webhooks, matched statement lines, credit, opening balance, tickets and their thread as the portal shows it, status history, notices, group membership, posts, comments, reactions and reports, messages sent to them, reminders, WhatsApp opt-ins, invitations, handover lines, consent records) and the original files of those rows. **The agency's internal notes on their tickets are left out** (its working notes, not shown to the tenant), though an erasure takes the tenant's details out of them.
10. **Anti-money-laundering flags are never exported** (to the tenant or the organization), because telling the subject of monitoring is tipping-off; the manifest says the table was left out.
11. **An organization's export holds every row of every table with its `organization_id`**, except GoProperty's support-session trail, its AML flags, consent records and the request register, and every file (originals, not thumbnails).
12. **Encrypted values are masked** to their last four characters (`•••• 8901`); encrypted lists (a compliance contact) show `••••`; passwords, keys, tokens and webhook secrets are never exported. Times are UTC, amounts kobo (the README says so).
13. **The archive** is a zip of `data/<table>.json`, `files/…`, `manifest.json` (rows per table, tables left out and why) and `README.txt`; built in the background on the `default` queue in 35-second runs that keep their place; kept **7 days** on the server's private `data-exports` disk (not in backups), then deleted; downloaded through a **15-minute signed link**, the permission checked again, every download audited.

**Erasure**

14. **Erasure is asked by staff only** (console, `privacy.requests`), on a verified request, and **always needs a second staff member's approval with `data.purge`** (PLATFORM_CONSOLE.md § 1 names organizations; a tenant's erasure destroys data too, so it follows the same rule). Owners ask GoProperty (the privacy page names the officer); the workspace offers no erasure.
15. **A tenant's erasure anonymises, it does not delete.** Their record becomes "Erased tenant", with no email, address, occupation, emergency contact or ID number, phone `erased-<id>`, no portal link, archived. Text they wrote (ticket messages, group posts and comments) becomes "[Removed at the person's request]"; their reactions and group membership are deleted.
16. **Their details are taken out of other text** — other people's messages and notes, statement narrations, issued documents' snapshots (`bills.details`, `receipts.details`, `lease_contracts.details`), message subjects — by replacing their full name (both orders), email, phone (in its written forms), address, ID number and emergency contact with "[erased]"; values shorter than four characters are not searched for (a two-letter name would erase unrelated words).
17. **Financial records keep their amounts, dates, numbers, statuses, payment references and links** (bills, payments, receipts, credit, deposits): the law's record without the person. The files of their records (proofs, receipts' PDFs, contracts, ticket attachments; evidence included) are purged inside `Media::purging()`, and the links to issued PDFs cleared.
18. **Consent records are kept** as the proof of consent, with the IP address and browser blanked.
19. **A tenant is erased only once no lease of theirs is a draft or active and no bill of theirs is unpaid, part paid or overdue** (the agency may still need to recover money), and **only 90 days after their last lease ended** (terminated, expired or its end date; their record's creation without a lease). Approving sets "erases from" to the later of now and that day.
20. **An organization is purged only once cancelled (its subscription ended) or deleted**, and only 90 days after the later of the two. Reactivated in between, the purge is cancelled when it would start. The same re-check stops a tenant's erasure if a new lease or an unpaid bill appeared after approval.
21. **The audit log is never rewritten** (append-only, hash-chained): erasure and purge entries carry numbers and counts only, never names, contacts or the reason staff typed; the request's own reason (and its approval's) loses the person's identifying values when the erasure runs. Entries written before an erasure keep their values until the audit retention runs out (see the open question below).
22. **A purge keeps GoProperty's own records** with the organization: the subscription and its billed units, platform invoices, payments and refunds (and the files they point at, such as a bank-transfer proof), the support-session trail, consent records (blanked) and the data request register. Everything else with its `organization_id` is deleted, files too, compliance documents included (the customer, not GoProperty, is the AML reporting entity).
23. **The purged organization stays as a tombstone row**: its name (on GoProperty's kept invoices), its slug `purged-<id>`, soft-deleted, its address, CAC number, branding and logo blanked.
24. **Sign-in accounts are anonymised only when nothing else uses them** (no membership, tenant or owner record left anywhere): name "Erased user", no email, phone, password anyone knows, two-factor secrets, passkeys, sessions, trusted devices, notifications, sign-in log or reset tokens. The row stays because records point at it. An account still used elsewhere is left alone.
25. **Email suppressions** of addresses nobody uses any more are lifted and blanked (DATABASE_SCHEMA.md "email_suppressions").
26. **The purge's delete order comes from the schema's foreign keys**: nullable links inside the organization are cleared first, another organization's nullable link to a purged row is cleared, and a required one stops the purge with the reason (nothing is half-deleted silently). It runs in 35-second steps that keep their place.
27. **`privacy-retention` runs every 15 minutes** (Schedule::call, shared hosting): queues approved erasures whose day has come, re-queues one a stopped worker left, deletes export files past their 7 days. So an erasure runs within 15 minutes of its day.
28. **An erasure can be withdrawn** by staff until it starts (Compliance → Data requests → Withdraw); a rejected or expired approval closes it.

**Breaches and the console**

29. **The breach register** is Console → Compliance → Breach register: title, what happened, when GoProperty became aware (the clock), when it happened, risk (unlikely / a risk / high), kinds of data, people affected, customers by number, consequences, measures; then Contained, Commission told (with its reference), Customers told, People told, No notice needed (with the reason) and Close (with what was learned). The 72-hour deadline shows as due, overdue (red) or done; the menu counts breaches still waiting for a Commission notice. Entries are never deleted.
30. **Breach permissions**: `privacy.breaches` for compliance-officer, technical-admin (who often finds breaches first) and super-admin.
31. **New staff permissions**, none protected: `privacy.requests` and `privacy.settings` (compliance-officer, super-admin), `privacy.breaches` (also technical-admin), area "Data protection" in the role editor. The erasure approver keeps `data.purge` (protected, super-admin).
32. **The console pages sit in existing menus**: Compliance → Data requests and Breach register; Settings → Data protection. No new navigation group.
33. **A breach's people and customers are kept as counts and organization numbers**, never names or contact details, and the register's audit entries carry the reference and status only.

### Open question for the product owner (P09-02)

- **Audit entries written before an erasure keep the person's details** (e.g. a tenant's name in a "tenant updated" entry), because the audit log is append-only and hash-chained (PLATFORM_CONSOLE.md § 7) and the NDPA asks for erasure. Today they stay until the audit retention (2 years, 7 for money) ends, which the NDPA allows where a record is kept to meet a legal obligation, and they are seen only by the organization's owners (Settings → Activity) and super-admins. If that is not enough, the next step would be to store the personal values in new audit entries encrypted per person (so an erasure can destroy the key), which needs its own task.

## P09-06 — Upgrades

Built by a task agent (2026-11-04): Console → Operations → **Version** and the super-admins' **Run pending migrations** step (`App\Installer\Upgrades`, `App\Platform\Filament\Pages\SystemVersion` / `SystemUpgrade`, `App\Http\Controllers\Console\UpgradeController`), maintenance mode for an upgrade that keeps the console open, and the upgrade drill (`scripts/release/upgrade-drill.sh`, workflow `upgrade-drill.yml`). Specification and how it is built: [INSTALLER.md § 10, § 12](../../INSTALLER.md); runbook [docs/runbooks/upgrade.md](../runbooks/upgrade.md). Acceptance: "Upgrade drill passes on both databases; one zip upgrade + rollback rehearsed on staging" (the staging rehearsal is for the product owner: upgrade.md § 5).

### Defaults taken in P09-06 (agent, 2026-11-04; for the product owner to confirm)

None of these changes a state machine (AGENT.md § 8): the upgrade step runs the release's own migrations and idempotent install seeders, as the Deploy workflow already does over SSH; it never edits customer data itself.

40. **Where and who:** "System → Version" is the console page Operations → **Version** (`/{ADMIN_PATH}/system/version`), for `system.status` (technical-admin, super-admin), read-only. The upgrade step is its own page (`/system/upgrade`, not in the menu), linked from Version while something is pending.
41. **New permission `system.upgrade`** ("Run an upgrade's migrations and maintenance mode from the console"): super-admins only, and **protected** (only a super-admin can add it to a role, with a second super-admin's approval), so "super-admin only" (INSTALLER.md § 10) holds while roles stay configurable. A super-admin holds it even before the upgrade's seeders have created its row (super-admins hold the whole catalogue in code), so the release that introduces it can be installed with it.
42. **Latest release:** GitHub's public `GET /repos/{owner}/{name}/releases/latest` for `GOPROPERTY_RELEASES_REPOSITORY` (default `thekingchild/Goproperty`): drafts and pre-releases are not shown; no token is stored, so a private repository shows "no published release yet (or the repository is private)". Only a link to the release's page on github.com is shown; asset (zip) links never are.
43. **When GitHub is asked:** only in production and staging (written-out list, as for every outside call), with 3 s to connect and 5 s in all; the answer is cached 6 hours, a failure 15 minutes, and shown in words ("GitHub could not be reached…"). **Check GitHub again** forgets the cached answer, 5 times per 10 minutes per staff member.
44. **What "pending" means:** migrations not run, or this release's install seeders not run yet. The latter compares the code's `VERSION` with `installation_state.version`; code without a release number ("dev": a checkout or a deploy archive) is never pending for seeders. A release that brings no migration (only, say, a new permission) is therefore still upgraded by the step.
45. **`installation_state.version` is now the release whose install seeders last ran** on a finalized installation: `InstalledVersionSeeder`, last in `InstallSeeder`, writes it (release numbers only; nothing during an install or without the row). So the Deploy workflow, `db:seed` after `migrate --force`, and the console step all keep it right. Before, it was the release that installed.
46. **Before the step:** the staff member's password and authenticator code (the console's `StepUp`, fresh for 10 minutes) and a tick that a database backup was taken before the new files went up. The tick is not checked against the backup list: a backup may be at a destination this site does not record, or the host's own; the page shows the last successful backup recorded here.
47. **One piece of work per request:** a batch of the installer's migrations (5 or 20 seconds, `installer.migrations_per_batch` / `batch_seconds`), or, once none is pending, the install seeders and the config, route, view and event caches, in a request of their own. The page posts the next request by itself after 250 ms (a button without JavaScript). The caches are rebuilt because the copied `storage/` holds the old release's compiled views, which Laravel would otherwise keep using.
48. **Two staff at once:** an exclusive file lock (`flock` on `storage/app/private/upgrade.lock`, taken without waiting), not a cache lock (the cache tables belong to the schema being changed); the second request is told "Another staff member is running the upgrade right now" and runs nothing.
49. **Maintenance mode during the step:** Laravel's maintenance mode with an upgrade marker in its payload (`UpgradeMaintenance`). While it carries the marker the console stays open — for every signed-in staff member with 2FA, not just the one upgrading — and Livewire's script loads; every other page answers 503. The step turns it on with its first piece of work when the site is not in maintenance mode, and off after the seeders and caches succeed, also when it was turned on from the Version page. A failure leaves it on. Maintenance mode from `php artisan down` is never changed by the console (it closes the console too).
50. **Maintenance switch on the Version page** (super-admins, step-up, audited `upgrade.maintenance_on` / `upgrade.maintenance_off`): only the upgrade kind, for step 4 of a no-SSH upgrade. This narrows P08-03 default 46 ("shown, not switched") for upgrades; the System operations App area still only shows it.
51. **Refused** when nothing is pending ("Nothing to run…"), with nothing changed and nothing audited.
52. **Audit** (log `operations`, the staff member as causer): `upgrade.maintenance_on`, `upgrade.migrated` (from, to, the migrations that ran, done of total), `upgrade.migration_failed` (migration and the error without connection details), `upgrade.finish_failed`, `upgrade.finished`, `upgrade.maintenance_off`.
53. **No rollback in the app:** the step only runs migrations forward. Rollback is the folder swap plus restoring the pre-upgrade backup (console **Restore to production**, two-person, or docs/runbooks/restore.md), never `migrate:rollback`.
54. **The previous release in the drill** (`scripts/release/previous-ref.sh`): the workflow's `previous` input; else the newest `v*` tag before this commit; else a pull request's base commit; else this commit's first parent. There are no tags yet, so pull requests upgrade from `main` and the nightly run from the commit before the last merge. It is built by `build-previous.sh` from that commit with **this** checkout's release scripts.
55. **The drill's backup and restore** are a copy of the database made with `CREATE TABLE … LIKE` and `INSERT … SELECT` on the same server, not the app's backup engine (which needs a destination and has drills of its own, P06-10). The rollback is rehearsed in CI on that copy; on staging with a real backup (upgrade.md § 5).
56. **The drill's data:** an agency, its owner, their membership and a setting inserted with SQL (version-independent), plus the install's staff account and audit log; compared before and after on the columns the first release had (`upgrade-fingerprint.php`), audit entries up to the last one before the upgrade.
57. **The drill without SSH** installs the previous release with the web wizard (how a no-SSH owner gets a console session), swaps the folders without maintenance mode first when the previous release has no Version page (true for this first one; the step turns maintenance on itself), and sets `INSTALLER_MIGRATIONS_PER_BATCH=1` (a new, optional setting; default 5) so it sees the step carry on over several requests.
58. **The upgrade drill workflow** runs nightly (03:00 UTC), on pull requests touching migrations, seeders, the installer, the upgrade step or the release scripts, by hand and before every release (the Release workflow waits for it). Like the install drill it is not a required check.
59. **The console pages the step needs** (sign-in, Version, Run pending migrations) run on the new code against the previous release's database until the migrations have run, so they must not depend on a newer table or column. A rule for developers, proven by the drill for every release. Navigation badges, drawn on every console page, count a table a release adds through `App\Platform\Filament\Concerns\BadgeBeforeMigrations` (nothing shown while the table is missing): the first drill after P09-02 found its two badges failing the Version page before its migrations had run (fixed 2026-11-04).
60. **Staging rehearsal** (one zip upgrade + rollback): not doable by the agent (no access to staging); the exact steps are docs/runbooks/upgrade.md § 5, for the product owner.
